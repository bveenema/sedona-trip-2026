4 bikes in a truck bed: use a tailgate pad (sized for 4–5 bikes) with fork-down over the tailgate, alternating directions, or pad between bikes with moving blankets. Strap each down tube to the pad and add a cam strap across the frames; ratchet straps can crush carbon. Remove loose items (bottles, bags, GPS), and check straps after the first few miles of I-17.

Backup
Thunder Mountain Bikes (West Sedona, closest), Absolute Bikes, Sedona Bike & Bean (Village of Oak Creek) for builds, tune-ups, or parts; call ahead
  • Tools: hex/Torx set, torque wrench (bars, stem, seatpost to spec), pedal wrench, shop rags, zip ties.
  • Suspension: shock pump; reset fork and shock pressures and sag.
  • Tires: floor or tire pump, sealant top-up, tubeless plugs, spare tube. Sedona rock is sharp; consider slightly higher pressure.
  • Drivetrain: check the derailleur hanger for bends from travel; confirm shifting across the cassette.
  • Brakes: pull the pad spacers, install wheels (don't squeeze levers without a rotor in), check rotors for bends, confirm firm lever feel.
  • Shakedown: short spin around the neighborhood or Thunder Mountain before a long ride.

Caves

Sedona's caves and cliff dwellings range from short roadside alcoves to full-day scrambles. Ranked here for casual hikers with no ropes or technical gear.

1. Birthing Cave
~2 mi RT from Long Canyon TH, flat approach with a short scramble into a rounded hollow. The left fork at ~0.6 mi is easy to miss. Best light noon–3 PM. Red Rock Pass required. Source.
Bikes: Allowed on Long Canyon Trail for the first 0.9 mi to the Red Rock-Secret Mountain Wilderness boundary; the Birthing Cave spur is at ~0.6 mi, before the boundary, so you can ride to the junction and lock up there.
2. Fay Canyon Arch
Flat, shady 2.4 mi trail with an unsigned spur on the right at ~0.5 mi that climbs steeply to an arch alcove. A few hands-on moves next to a 15–25 ft drop. Red Rock Pass required. Source.
Bikes: Not allowed. Fay Canyon Trail is entirely within the Red Rock-Secret Mountain Wilderness, where bicycles are prohibited.
3. Honanki Heritage Site
Cliff dwellings with no scrambling and no reservation. Open 9:30–3, Red Rock Pass required. Rough dirt road (~40 min from West Sedona via Forest Road 525), and it closes after rain. Source.
Bikes: Not a mountain bike destination. Accessed via ~9.5 mi of rough dirt forest roads; the heritage site trails are short walking paths. No nearby legal singletrack.
4. Palatki Heritage Site
Cliff dwellings and rock-art alcoves. A timed tour reservation is required (book here) plus a Red Rock Pass. Last tour at 2 PM; it closes after rain. Reached via ~7 mi of dirt forest roads (FR 525 then FR 795).
Bikes: Not a mountain bike destination. Accessed via rough dirt forest roads; the heritage site trails are short walking paths. No nearby legal singletrack.
5. Soldier Pass Cave
~4.5 mi RT with a steep spur and an upper-body scramble into the cave, and often a line. Thu–Sun the trailhead lot is closed and you take the free Route 14 shuttle from Posse Grounds; the last return bus is 5:08 PM. Mon–Tue the lot opens at 8 AM with ~14 spots. Red Rock Pass required. Source.
Bikes: Not allowed to the cave. Bikes are permitted on the first 1.3 mi of Soldier Pass Trail to the wilderness boundary, but the cave spur is at ~1.4 mi, inside the wilderness where bikes are prohibited. Hike from the trailhead.
6. Subway Cave (Boynton Canyon)
~5 mi RT (to the cave and back) with a slippery slickrock climb at the end. Turn left at the big dead tree at ~2 mi. Don't touch or climb the ruins. Red Rock Pass required. Source.
Bikes: Not allowed. Boynton Canyon Trail enters the Red Rock-Secret Mountain Wilderness immediately after the trailhead, where bicycles are prohibited.

November timing: sunset ~5:25 PM, so plan to be off scrambles by ~4:30 PM for daylight. Wet or frosty slickrock is very slick; skip technical climbs if trails are damp.

Stargazing

Sedona is an officially certified International Dark Sky Community, so the city keeps outdoor lighting low and shielded. High-desert elevation and dry, clear air keep the Milky Way easy to see.

Three easy DIY spots

  • Airport Mesa — a short drive up from West Sedona, near the Airbnb, with wide-open views over town.
  • Two Trees — on Forest Road 525 off Highway 89A, a dark, open area local astronomy groups use for viewing.
  • Bell Rock Pathway — near the Village of Oak Creek; flat and easy to walk at night with headlamps, with a big open sky.
Moon timing: new moon is around Nov 9, so during the Nov 12–17 trip the moon is a thin crescent growing toward first quarter. It sets in the evening, earlier at the start of the week and later toward the end, so skies get dark after moonset, especially the first couple of nights. Note: moon dates are calculated; double-check before going.

Airport Mesa and Bell Rock Pathway require a Red Rock Pass (buy online).

Red Rock Pass

A Red Rock Pass ($5/day, $15/week, $20/year) is required to park at popular Coconino National Forest trailheads and viewpoints in the Sedona area, including Airport Mesa, Bell Rock Pathway, and most mountain bike trailheads. The fees help maintain trails, facilities, and the area's natural resources.

Buy online at Recreation.gov (your license plate is registered digitally, no printed pass needed), or purchase at trailhead kiosks or local vendors. The pass covers the passholder's vehicle and all occupants at designated fee sites.

Good to know

Mid-November: the Nov 6–8 bike festival is the weekend before, so crowds should be lighter. Cool mornings, highs around the 60s, sunset near 5:20 PM: start rides by 9 AM and finish before dark.

The free Sedona Shuttle runs Thu–Sun to Cathedral Rock, Little Horse, Dry Creek Vista and Mescal trailheads, with only 3 bike racks per bus; the Cathedral Rock lot is closed to cars on shuttle days. No e-bikes on singletrack. Check current closures before the trip.
