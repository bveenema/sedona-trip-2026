That gives 4 full days in Sedona (Fri–Mon). Plan on leaving Sedona around 5:30 AM Tuesday for ~9 AM departures; adjust once everyone's flights are set.

4 bikes in a truck bed: use a tailgate pad (sized for 4–5 bikes) with fork-down over the tailgate, alternating directions, or pad between bikes with moving blankets. Strap each down tube to the pad and add a cam strap across the frames; ratchet straps can crush carbon. Remove loose items (bottles, bags, GPS), and check straps after the first few miles of I-17.

Bike build and teardown

Build
Thursday evening at the house or Friday morning before riding; about 1.5–2.5 hr for 4 bikes with 1–2 people building
Teardown
Monday evening: clean, pack, and box/bag bikes before the early Tuesday departure (allow 1.5–2 hr)
Backup
Thunder Mountain Bikes (West Sedona, closest), Absolute Bikes, Sedona Bike & Bean (Village of Oak Creek) for builds, tune-ups, or parts; call ahead
  • Tools: hex/Torx set, torque wrench (bars, stem, seatpost to spec), pedal wrench, shop rags, zip ties.
  • Suspension: shock pump; reset fork and shock pressures and sag.
  • Tires: floor or tire pump, sealant top-up, tubeless plugs, spare tube. Sedona rock is sharp; consider slightly higher pressure.
  • Drivetrain: check the derailleur hanger for bends from travel; confirm shifting across the cassette.
  • Brakes: pull the pad spacers, install wheels (don't squeeze levers without a rotor in), check rotors for bends, confirm firm lever feel.
  • Shakedown: short spin around the neighborhood or Thunder Mountain before a long ride.

Good to know

Mid-November: the Nov 6–8 bike festival is the weekend before, so crowds should be lighter. Cool mornings, highs around the 60s, sunset near 5:20 PM: start rides by 9 AM and finish before dark.

The free Sedona Shuttle runs Thu–Sun to Cathedral Rock, Little Horse, Dry Creek Vista and Mescal trailheads, with only 3 bike racks per bus; the Cathedral Rock lot is closed to cars on shuttle days. No e-bikes on singletrack. Check current closures before the trip.
